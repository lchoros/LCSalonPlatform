## Decisions made
- **Next was the correction of a wrong method**, because it is the commonest mistake at a till, it makes the
  cash-up wrong, and ADR 0060 and ADR 0061 both named it as not built.
- **The line is never edited; two lines move its amount** from the method it was recorded under to the method it
  was paid by (ADR 0064). The takings of the day stay what they were. Everything that adds money up by method was
  right without being changed.
- **Only a line of today.** A correction counts on the till of the day it is made, so one made today for an older
  line would change today's drawer. Backdating a ledger line was rejected.
- **Allowed after the day was closed**, because the count is often how the mistake is found. The count stands and
  the till says what the drawer should hold now, as it already did for money recorded after a close.
- **A page per line, not a form per row.** Each line of today's till has a link; a till of thirty lines with
  thirty lists would not be usable on a phone.
- **Every page that says how something was paid says the corrected method**, through one helper.
- **It does not change an amount.** A wrong amount is a refund, with its own rules, and is not built.

## What was learned
- **A race test can pass for the wrong reason.** The first version sent six corrections of one payment with
  `Task.Run` and passed with the lock taken out: the requests did not overlap. The test now makes every request do
  a first read, holds them all at a gate, and lets 24 go at the same moment, three on each of eight payments.
  That version fails every time without the lock. A race test is only believed after it has been seen to fail.
- Two entities made in the same millisecond have ids in no particular order: a UUIDv7 is random below the
  millisecond. Where two lines written together must read in an order, the second id is made one millisecond on.
