## How it was built
All of it solo, in one worktree and one migration lineage (`ClientAccountEntries`, `Deposits`, `CheckoutAndCashUp`,
`OutboxCompletedConsumers`), so no two branches ever added a migration side by side. Stacking instead of waiting kept
the work moving while Tier 2 PRs wait for the owner; nothing was merged by an agent.

## Decisions made (each in its ADR as "owner may veto")
- **The policy proposes, a person decides** (0022). No fee is ever added automatically, not even for a late online
  cancellation. Marking a no-show or a late cancellation leads to a preview; the desk adds, lowers or skips the fee.
- **Online cut-off** (0022, approved by the owner as "applies to both"): inside the notice period a client can no
  longer *change* the time online; *cancelling* stays possible with the fee stated first, because blocking it would
  turn late cancellations into no-shows.
- **One append-only ledger per client** (0022 to 0024): fees, what settled them, deposits, their returns and visit
  payments are lines that are never edited. "One fee, one deposit and one payment per visit" and "one settlement per
  fee" are unique indexes, so a double tap is refused by the database.
- **Deposit is a zadatek** (0023): client rule over service rule over salon rule, zero opts out; snapshot at booking;
  a deposit the salon holds counts toward the fee of a missed visit and comes off the bill of one that took place.
- **The till is a view of the ledger** (0024): the day per method is the sum of the lines that carry a method, so the
  M2 gate ("deposits, no-show fees and the cash-up add up") holds by construction. A day is closed once with the cash
  counted; later entries are flagged, not blocked.
- **Delivery per consumer** (0025): the outbox row lists the consumers that are done; every consumer gets its turn
  and only the failed one is retried. A column on the row instead of an inbox table.

## What was learned
- `dotnet ef migrations remove --no-build` straight after `migrations add --no-build` removes the *previous*
  migration, because the stale assembly does not know the new one. It took out a committed migration locally; restored
  from git before anything was pushed. Redo a fresh migration by deleting its two files and checking out the snapshot.
- NodaTime's `LocalDateTime` rejects the "d" and "t" format shortcuts. Two views crashed only when they had rows to
  show; page tests must render pages with data in them, not only empty states.
- A test for "a failed consumer does not repeat the others" showed that the first design still let one failing
  consumer hold up the rest for that attempt. The dispatcher now runs every consumer and collects the failures.
- On Windows `dotnet format --verify-no-changes` flags two line breaks in one file that Linux CI accepts; CI is the
  authority, the file is left alone.
